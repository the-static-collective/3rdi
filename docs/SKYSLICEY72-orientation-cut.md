## Y as a local orientation frame

For a three-arm Y, the full orientation grammar is:

```text
D3 ≅ S3
|D3| = 6
```

with rotation `r` and reflection `f` satisfying:

```text
r^3 = e
f^2 = e
f r f = r^-1
```

Thus:

```text
turn != twist
```

and in general:

```text
turn then flip != flip then turn
```

This gives a compact six-state local-frame toy for projection experiments.

## Fiber-optic cut metaphor

The useful systems grammar is:

```text
carrier
-> oriented aperture / cut
-> observer-facing projection
```

3rdi does not claim optical equivalence. It uses the specimen to pressure the rule:

> **THE APERTURE THAT PRODUCED THE PROJECTION IS PART OF THE PROJECTION RECEIPT.**

## Handedness and observer blindness

A coarse observer may retain endpoint arrangement while losing crossing handedness. A richer later observer may distinguish the histories only if the necessary formation was preserved upstream.

Therefore:

```text
later visibility != earlier availability
later discrimination != retroactive earlier perception
```

The six Y orientations can be used as a hostile lab only after the local frame is declared before projection.
