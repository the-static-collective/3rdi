# 3rdi Operator Field Guide

`3rdi` is the eye you use when **where/when/from-whom you look changes what can honestly be said**.

It is not an oracle. It is an aperture with receipts.

## The feel of the instrument

A good `3rdi` run should feel like moving a camera without moving the landscape.

The field may contain more than the selected observer can see. The engine may know more than the historical cut may use. A later decoder may reveal more structure than an earlier decoder could produce. None of those facts grants permission to rewrite the earlier world.

The constitutional move is therefore simple:

> Move the aperture. Preserve the carrier. Receipt the difference.

## Wake it when

Reach for `3rdi` when at least one of these is load-bearing:

- **Temporal locality:** “What could they have known *then*?”
- **Observer locality:** two people can lawfully inhabit different information worlds over one anchored occurrence.
- **Audience locality:** information exists but was not yet exposed through this layer.
- **Decoder locality:** the same carrier yields different projections under declared receiver constitutions.
- **Relation birthdays:** an edge becomes perceived or relevant later and must not be backdated.
- **Reinterpretation:** a later reading should descend from, not overwrite, the earlier one.
- **Hindsight danger:** the answer gets easier only because the current operator can see the ending.
- **Authority danger:** a visibility or gate result is being mistaken for permission to act.

## Leave it sleeping when

Do not invoke `3rdi` merely because a task contains dates, graphs, observers, symbolism, or the word “perspective.”

Usually stay with ordinary tools for:

- summarizing a source as it exists now;
- listing events in chronological order;
- drawing or analyzing a graph where observer-specific availability is irrelevant;
- comparing interpretations without a carrier/decoder distinction;
- generating a metaphor or hypothesis that does not claim historical knowability;
- proving whether a spiritual, symbolic, or metaphysical claim is true.

`3rdi` can receipt such a claim’s provenance or observer-local availability. It cannot promote the claim into evidence by projecting it cleanly.

## Five eyes

### `CUT` — “What is visible here?”

Use the smallest lawful world. Name the observer, `focus_at`, `known_at`, admitted audience layers, and source cut. If the observer cannot lawfully receive something, keep the hidden category in the audit receipt without leaking its content into the observer view.

### `PARALLAX` — “Why do these worlds differ?”

Compare receipts over one stable field. Prefer this over arguing which observer is “really right” when both may be locally correct.

Useful comparison axes:

```text
observer | audience | focus_at | known_at | location | decoder
```

Change one axis at a time when possible.

### `REINTERPRET` — “What changed in the receiver?”

Keep the carrier digest stable. Declare the new decoder. Emit a new projection and formation trace. Never edit the old projection into having always meant the new thing.

### `GATE` — “Does this enter this projection?”

A gate returns:

```text
pass | fail | unresolved
```

with reasons and evidence. Stop there. If a write/send/admit/authorize action is desired, hand the receipt to a separately authorized adapter.

### `LAB` — “What would embarrass this interpretation?”

The best pressure test is usually boring.

Try one:

- cut before the decisive evidence arrived;
- same occurrence, second observer;
- same carrier, different declared decoder;
- shuffled chronology with labels untouched;
- later relevance while the causal ledger must remain byte-stable;
- remove the evocative vocabulary and see whether the structure survives.

If the claim dies when the poetry is removed, receipt that result.

## The four clocks

Never store “time” as one overloaded thing when these differ:

| Coordinate | Question |
|---|---|
| `occurred_at` | When did the occurrence happen? |
| `available_from` | When could this observer/layer receive it? |
| `focus_at` | Where is the aperture looking? |
| `known_at` | How much availability history may this cut use? |

A historical cut normally requires `known_at <= focus_at`.

A reconstruction may use later receipts, but must mark them as hindsight-bearing and still withhold actual future occurrences from the reconstructed observer view.

## Edge birthdays

An edge is not born merely because two nodes existed.

Keep:

```text
edge identity
first perceived time
discovery trace
assessment history
observer/audience exposure
class = causal | relevance
```

Later relevance may create a new relevance edge. It may not repaint an old causal ledger.

## Receiver discipline

When decoding, make this chain attributable:

```text
CARRIER -> DECODER RECEIPT -> PROJECTION -> FORMATION TRACE
```

If two decoders disagree, that is often the result—not a bug to average away.

Ask:

1. Is the carrier actually the same?
2. Is each decoder constitution declared?
3. Did the projection change because the receiver changed?
4. Is either projection being mistaken for source authority?

## A useful final receipt

Lead with the answer the caller wanted. Then expose only the machinery needed to audit it:

1. **Projection / comparison**
2. **Cut** — observer, `focus_at`, `known_at`, audience/location scope
3. **Decoder receipts** — when applicable
4. **Causal ledger** and **relevance ledger** separately
5. **Gate states**
6. **Hindsight / freshness / non-authority notices**
7. **Withheld categories and residual fog**
8. **Pressure result** — what control was tried and whether the claim survived

Do not bury the result under ontology.

## Operator maxim

> **A cleaner projection is not a truer source. A later revelation is not an earlier cause. A visible gate is not an open door.**
